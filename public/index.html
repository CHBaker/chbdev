Shostak: three apps in six months, inside a capped budget

A streaming platform for independent filmmakers. A Briebug client project. I was the solution engineering lead and the technical lead.

The client wanted to work with us and hesitated on cost. I fit the scope to a negotiated cap and kept the cap visible, so every trade-off could be argued in the open. Sprint 0 meant the team was building product on day one instead of wiring up tools.

We shipped a creator platform, a viewer app, and an admin app. The viewer app browses like a streaming service: worlds, series, seasons, episodes, then the player. Discover Worlds is its front door, with search, genre and tier filters, and a recommendation algorithm that ranks the content. I designed the solution for the admin billing system and the creator revenue-share logic, and led the team that built them. For a stretch late in the build, I was the only engineer.

  • Delivered inside the cap, with a larger epic than we first agreed.
  • Three major releases.
  • A launch announced at the Sundance Film Festival.

Creator platform

Where filmmakers publish and manage their work. An upload starts by choosing a content tier, and each tier says what it is for.

Viewer app

Where audiences watch. Discover Worlds ranks the catalog with a recommendation algorithm, alongside search and genre and tier filters. From there it browses like a streaming service: worlds, series, seasons, episodes, then the player. It reflows for phones.

Admin app

Runs the platform behind the scenes, including the billing system and the creator revenue-share logic. It stays private.

The invoice that nearly doubled

A hand-off pushed a client's invoice to roughly double the original bid. I found the cause, corrected the engineer's practice, set up billing controls for that client, and negotiated the resolution. They did two more feature cycles and later signed a quarter-long engagement.

Read the story
The situation

The client had a small budget and was billed ticket by ticket. An engineer who took over another engineer's work ran well over the original bid. The invoice came in at roughly twice what we had quoted, and the client was shocked.

What I did

I started by finding out why, before anyone argued about money. The cause was how the engineer who took over was working, so I corrected that practice. Then I set up billing controls for that client so a surprise like that could not happen again. Last, I sat down with the client and their project manager. I negotiated a reduced invoice and walked them through the reasoning behind the part that stayed.

How it ended

The client's trust came back, and it did not happen again. They went on to do two more feature cycles with us and later signed a quarter-long engagement.
